Unit 1: Area and Surface Area

Units

Grade 6 Unit 1

Area and surface area start with moving pieces, not memorizing formulas.

Unit 1 builds from tiling and rearranging regions into parallelograms, triangles, polygons, nets, polyhedra, squares, cubes, surface area, and volume.

1. Rearrange area 2. Parallelograms 3. Triangles 4. Polygons 5. Surface area 6. Squares and cubes 7. Tent design

Idea 1 (Unit 1, Lessons 1-3)

Equal-area pieces can be moved without changing area.

Counting pieces is only safe when the pieces are the same size. This model shows how rhombuses and trapezoids can be compared using one triangle as the area unit.

Idea 2 (Unit 1, Lessons 4-6)

A parallelogram becomes a rectangle with the same base and height.

The slanted side is not the height. The height is the perpendicular distance from the chosen base to the opposite side.

Idea 3 (Unit 1, Lessons 7-10)

Two matching triangles make one parallelogram.

A triangle with base b and height h has half the area of the related parallelogram.

Idea 4 (Unit 1, Lesson 11)

Polygons can be split into shapes whose areas we already know.

A good decomposition keeps the pieces visible and makes the calculation match the visual structure.

Idea 5 (Unit 1, Lessons 12-15)

Surface area is the sum of every face area.

A net keeps each face visible. Opposite faces match, but they still count as separate faces on the solid.

Idea 6 (Unit 1, Lessons 16-18)

Squared, cubed, surface area, and volume measure different things.

An edge length can create a square face, a cube volume, or six square faces. The exponent tells what kind of measure is being built.

Idea 7 (Unit 1, Lesson 19)

A design problem needs the faces that use material.

The tent task asks students to decide which panels matter, estimate, and explain what measurements are needed.
